Austin sits in Hail Alley. Here's what that means for your roof.

Central Texas takes some of the most frequent, most damaging hail in the country. A little preparation makes an enormous difference in what a storm costs you.

Meteorologists call the corridor from Texas up through the plains "Hail Alley," and Central Texas sits squarely inside it. The September 2023 storm caused roughly $300 million in damage across Williamson County alone, and the May 2025 storm dropped golf-ball hail across East Austin. This is not occasional bad luck. It is a recurring season, and your roof is the part of your home that takes the hit.

What hail actually does to a roof

Hail bruises shingles, knocks granules loose, cracks mats, and dents soft metals like vents and flashing. Much of this is invisible from the ground, and a bruised roof can look fine for months before leaks start. That delay is why Texas gives homeowners generally up to 2 years to file a storm claim.

What homeowners can do

  • After any hailstorm, check gutters, downspouts, and window screens for dents, and look for shingle granules collecting at downspout exits.
  • Photograph hail on the ground next to a coin or tape measure, and note the date and time of the storm.
  • Get a professional inspection before signing anything with an out-of-town crew. Out-of-town crews arrive with the weather and leave with it.
  • Ask about impact-resistant Class 4 shingles when you replace. They stand up to hail better, and many Texas insurers offer premium discounts for them. Read our guide on Class 4 upgrades.
